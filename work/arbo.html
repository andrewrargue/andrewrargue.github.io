Case study

Arbo — Illustration & Visual Direction

Navigating client vision, budget reality, and creative collaboration to deliver a cohesive visual system.

Client

Arbo

Role

UI & Visual Direction Lead

Year

2025

Team

Patricia Orozco (Art Director)

Overview

Arbo turns obscure energy regulatory data into infrastructure and business intelligence — influencing billions of dollars in infrastructure investment decisions. As part of a broader rebrand engagement, Arbo needed a cohesive illustration and visual system that matched the sophistication of their platform and audience.

The existing illustration library was scattered and inconsistent — cartoon-like characters incongruent with a platform trusted by ExxonMobil, TC Energy, and Tellurian. The engagement required not just new illustrations, but a new visual language and a process model that could manage an ambitious client vision within a real budget.

Disciplines

Illustration Visual Design Client Collaboration

Challenges

  • Illustration style signalled startup, not enterprise
  • No unifying system — scattered styles, abstraction levels, and references
  • Client vision exceeded what the budget could support
  • Feedback cycles were open-ended without a shared system to evaluate against
  • Needed to deliver at the quality level Arbo expected within a fixed scope

The problem

Cartoon-like illustrations on a platform trusted by ExxonMobil.

The existing illustration library was scattered and inconsistent — a mix of styles, levels of abstraction, and visual references with no unifying system. The illustration style itself was incongruent with a platform trusted by ExxonMobil, TC Energy, and Tellurian.

Visitors encountering the illustrations got a signal that didn't match the seriousness of the product underneath. For a company influencing billions of dollars in infrastructure investment decisions, the visual language was working against the brand.

The illustrations were telling visitors a different story than the product.

This is the final web solution design.

The client challenge

A highly specific vision that exceeded what the budget could support.

Arbo arrived with a highly specific, detail-driven vision for illustration — a level of customisation and scope that exceeded what the engagement's budget could support. The client's enthusiasm and attention to detail were genuine assets, but left unmanaged they would have derailed the project timeline and blown past what was scoped.

Feedback cycles became inefficient as a result — revisions on individual illustrations without a shared system to evaluate them against meant every round could open new directions rather than converge toward a final system.

The collaboration

Bring the client further into the process, not further away from it.

Patricia and I made a deliberate pivot: rather than pushing back on Arbo's vision or quietly scaling down to fit budget, we brought the client further into the process. We ran collaborative direction-setting sessions together — working through reference points, abstraction levels, and colour systems as a shared exercise rather than a one-way deliverable.

This gave Arbo's team a real stake in the direction, which made the eventual system feel co-created rather than imposed. It also let us establish the system first — before working through individual illustrations — which dramatically reduced the open-ended feedback problem.

The result was a scope that fit the budget without feeling like a compromise to the client.

Key decision — System first

Define the visual language before producing a single illustration.

Replaced the scattered illustration library with a single cohesive system: dark-background, geometric, data-driven visual language. Palette, abstraction level, and composition rules were all defined before any individual illustrations were produced.

Establishing the system first kept feedback convergent — every round of revisions was evaluated against a shared reference, not an open-ended creative brief. It also meant the illustrator could work efficiently within clear constraints rather than interpreting a loose direction differently each time.

Key decision — Applied system

A visual identity appropriate to energy infrastructure professionals.

The resulting system uses geometric forms, a defined palette drawn from Arbo's brand colours, and consistent metaphor vocabulary around pipelines, data flows, and energy infrastructure — building visual language that speaks directly to Arbo's audience rather than generic SaaS conventions.

Applied across the site, the illustration system gives Arbo a distinct visual identity that reinforces rather than undermines the seriousness of the platform.

The outcome

They didn't just approve the final work — they were genuinely excited about it.

Approval came quickly once the system was established. The collaborative process model — establish the system first, share concepts, then produce individual piece proofs third — turned a scope that could have felt like a limitation into a framework the client felt ownership over.

The process model itself became a repeatable approach for managing ambitious client visions within real budget constraints — applicable beyond this engagement to future projects where client enthusiasm and scope need to be brought into alignment without dampening either.

Results

Co-created Direction developed collaboratively with Arbo's team, not delivered to them
System-first Palette, abstraction, and composition rules defined before any illustrations
Repeatable Process model now used for managing ambitious client visions within real budgets
